Abstract—This proposed project investigates an AI-based software platform that assists patient case-taking in outpatient departments (OPDs) before the doctor's consultation. Patient history taking is a critical component of clinical consultation; limited consultation time is a constraint, incomplete patient recall, unstructured medical records, and language or literacy barriers. This paper proposes RogMitra, that conducts an adaptive history-taking interaction through voice and touch-based interfaces, digitizes existing medical documents, and generates a structured, physician-editable clinical history summary. The system is intended for documentation support and does not perform autonomous diagnosis. An extended Ayurvedic history mode supports AYUSH settings. I. PROBLEM DOMAIN IDENTIFICATION AND ANALYSIS
A. Background
History taking is the structured process of collecting information regarding a patient's presenting complaints, history of present illness, past medical and surgical history, medication and allergy history, family and personal history, and review of systems. It is widely regarded as one of the most important diagnostic activities in clinical medicine. The Indian 
Government Hospital OPDs have very limited time for this. Large tertiary hospitals may receive thousands of OPD patients each day, resulting in short consultation windows [1]. Within this window, the physician must elicit history, examine the patient, review prior records, decide on management, and counsel the patient. This can lead to incomplete history, missed comorbidities, and repeated questioning across visits.
The challenge becomes more complex in AYUSH and Ayurvedic healthcare settings, where patient assessment may require additional information beyond conventional clinical history. Parameters such as Prakriti, Vikriti, Agni, Koshta, and Aahara-Vihara may need to be documented along with symptoms, previous treatments, laboratory reports, and other relevant clinical information, which need a much more detailed history than routine intake [2]. Parameters such as Prakriti, Vikriti, Agni, Koshta, and Aahara-Vihara may need to be documented along with symptoms, previous treatments, laboratory reports, and other relevant clinical information. These records may exist in handwritten or paper-based formats and may be documented using different languages, making manual organization and retrieval time-consuming. 
The Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) has established a framework for digital health records and interoperability through components such as ABHA and FHIR-based healthcare information 
exchange. However, a patient-facing system specifically designed to collect structured clinical history and organize existing medical documents before the physician consultation remains an important area for exploration.
RogMitra is proposed in this context. The idea is to shift part of the information collection process to the period before the actual consultation. The patient can interact with the system using voice or touch, while the consultant receives a structured summary that can be reviewed before continuing with the further consultation. 
B. Problem Statement
We want to design RogMitra—a smart software platform where patients records their medical history through voice conversation or simple touch screens. It digitizes old paper documents and creates an editable summary for doctors before consultation starts. The system will collect relevant clinical information, processes existing medical documents , extracts useful information from them which can further be used for consultation and organize the information into a structured history. It will further generate a concise clinical summary for the consultant. Plus, it links seamlessly with hospital systems and ABHA records.
The main goal is not to automate diagnosis, but to focus on making the initial history taking process and documentation a bit more organized.
C. Problem Analysis
The main issues identified in the existing process are given below.
• A physician may not have enough time to collect a complete medical history manually during a busy OPD session.
• Previous prescriptions, laboratory reports, discharge summaries, and handwritten notes may exist in different formats and locations.
• Ayurvedic evaluation takes too long and fails in fast-paced OPD settings.
• Current registration systems only collect basic details like name and token number.
• Many OPD patients are elderly, rural or low-literacy users, so a text-only or app-based tool may not be usable.
• Information often is written or entered manually even when similar information has already been provided in earlier consultations.
• Health data must be handled with consent and privacy safeguards under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the ABDM consent framework [3].
D. Scope
The scope of RogMitra is limited to pre-consultation patient history collection and clinical documentation.
The proposed system will cover voice and touch based history interview, red-flag alerting, an Ayurvedic history mode, document digitization with entity extraction, structured summary generation, and consent and FHIR-based integration design. It will not diagnose diseases, prescribe treatment or replace the physician. All summaries are drafts that the physician can accept, edit or reject.
For AYUSH-oriented settings, the system will include an extended history module covering relevant Ayurvedic parameters.
RogMitra will not be diagnosing diseases or prescribing medicines. Neither will it make decisions on behalf of the consultant. The output generated by the system will be treated as a draft and will require physician review before it is used as part of clinical documentation. B. Research / Technology Gap
Existing tools focus on diagnosis or plain records. Nobody brings together (i) voice and touch for low-literacy users, (ii) Ayurvedic intake, (iii) OCR digitization of messy papers, and (iv) ABHA/FHIR integration in one single workflow. That’s the exact gap RogMitra fills. The proposed approach therefore combines four major areas:
Voice and touch interaction for patients with different levels of literacy and technological familiarity. 
Adaptive history taking, where subsequent questions can depend on the information already provided by the patient. 
Medical document digitization, allowing information from existing prescriptions, reports, and other records to be extracted and organized. 
Structured clinical summarization, where the collected information is converted into a concise format that can be reviewed by the physician.
Whether the proposed approach gives useful results will be examined experimentally rather than assumed.

III. Proposed System Design
A. Architecture Overview RogMitra follows a modular pipeline with five layers:
Interaction layer: This is where the patient interacts. It uses multilingual voice and touchscreens along with text-to-speech prompts.
Conversation engine: An adaptive questioning module. It uses a clinical history ontology to decide what to ask next based on old responses, plus it raises red-flag alerts if something looks dangerous.
Document intelligence module: Patients can upload or scan their previous medical records through the system. OCR is used to convert these documents into machine-readable text. The system then identifies useful information such as medicines, dosages, diagnoses, laboratory values, and dates.
Summary generator: It takes everything from the interview and scanned documents to create a structured draft summary that doctors can easily edit.
Integration and consent layer: This layer handles patient consent and the exchange of structured health information. The proposed design considers ABHA linking, FHIR-based resource mapping, and possible integration with hospital information systems (HIS). Any exchange of patient information would be subject to appropriate consent, authorization, and security requirements.
B. Patient and Physician Workflow
First, the patient registers and gives consent on the system. They complete the voice interview in whatever language they are comfortable in, scan their old paper records, and check a simple confirmation screen. Once done, the physician sees a structured draft summary on their screen with critical red flags highlighted. The doctor can quickly accept, edit, or reject the note before saving it into the system.
C. Ayurvedic History Mode For AYUSH and Ayurvedic consultations, RogMitra can operate in an extended history-taking mode. In this mode, the conversation engine uses an additional question bank designed around Ayurvedic assessment requirements. The question set can cover areas such as Dashavidha Pariksha, Prakriti, Vikriti, Agni, Koshtha, and Ahara-Vihara. The questions can be presented gradually instead of asking the patient to provide all the information at once. This makes the interaction easier to follow and allows later questions to depend on earlier responses. The Ayurvedic question bank and its structure should be reviewed and validated by a qualified Ayurvedic practitioner before being used for clinical purposes.
D. Data Design and Interoperability
All interview answers and extracted entities gets mapped directly into standard FHIR resources—such as Patient, Condition, Allergy Intolerance, Medication Statement, and Observation. This makes sure the data is fully aligned with ABDM standards and can be shared across health systems easily.
IV. OBJECTIVES AND METHODOLOGY OF THE PROPOSED WORK
A. Objectives
The proposed work focuses on developing RogMitra as a pre-consultation system that can collect patient information, process previous medical records, and prepare a structured history for the physician. The main objectives are:
Figure out why clinical history-taking is such a massive bottleneck in Indian OPDs.
Set up an instant red-flag alert system for emergency symptoms.
To design an adaptive voice- and touch-based history-taking system using a structured clinical history ontology.
To develop a document-processing pipeline that can digitize, extract, and organize information from previous medical records.
Develop a summary generator that outputs a clean, editable note for doctors.
Build a smart voice and touchscreen interview setup based on a proper clinical ontology.
B. Proposed Methodology
Data Collection: The project will use multiple datasets for different parts of the system. MTS-Dialog will be used for training the clinical summary-generation component, while ACI-Bench will be used for external evaluation. Synthea [10] will be used to generate synthetic patient profiles, medical records, and FHIR-format data.
Most of these datasets are based on English-language and Western clinical settings. Therefore, Hindi and Ayurvedic examples will be generated synthetically for the relevant modules. These examples will be reviewed and validated by a domain expert to improve their clinical and contextual relevance.
Preprocessing: Dialogue-note pairs will be cleaned and mapped to a common section format. ACI-Bench SOAP-style notes will be mapped to the history sections used in this project for evaluation.
Speech input is converted to text by ASR. A dialogue manager tracks which history fields are filled and asks the next relevant question. Symptom-specific follow-ups follow the SOCRATES framework. Every question can be answered by speaking or tapping.
Uploaded documents are processed by OCR, and an LLM extracts diagnoses, medicines with dosage, investigation values and procedures into a fixed JSON schema. Dates are used to build a timeline, and values outside reference ranges are highlighted.
A sequence-to-sequence model or LoRA-tuned LLM generates the structured summary from the conversation and extracted document data.
Patient authentication through ABHA, granular consent, FHIR mapping of the summary, and deletion of temporary session data after submission.
Evaluation: We will measure summary quality, ASR performance, entity extraction accuracy, and overall history coverage.

• Physical review: Physicians will review the sample of summaries, wherever possible.
